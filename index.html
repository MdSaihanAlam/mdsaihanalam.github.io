Papaya leaf disease detection with an in-browser vision transformer

A MaxViT-Tiny model fine-tuned on 4,436 labelled leaf images to recognise anthracnose, black spot, mealybug infestation, papaya mosaic virus, papaya ring spot virus and healthy leaves, then compressed until it runs inside a web browser.

Model variantSizeTest accuracyPredictions changed
PyTorch FP32trained checkpoint122.6 MB97.75%reference
ONNX FP16deployed in the browser62.2 MB97.75%0 of 666
ONNX INT8static quantisation, rejected–28.7%accuracy collapsed
 Held-out test set of 666 images. Macro-F1 0.976; best validation accuracy 99.10%.
  • Half the size, identical answers. FP16 compression halved the model with zero prediction changes, while 8-bit quantisation broke this attention-based architecture.
  • Pixel-exact preprocessing in JavaScript. PIL resampling, centre-crop rounding, EXIF rotation and normalisation were re-implemented so the browser matches Python on 666 of 666 test images, in about 0.6 s per image on a laptop CPU.

Convolutional, transformer and multimodal models for guava leaf and fruit disease

Six architectures trained and tested with one recipe on 3,300 leaf images (six classes) and 902 fruit images (four classes), so the comparison is fair.

 Test sets of 487 leaf and 131 fruit images. Best fruit result 78.63% (95% CI 70.8 to 84.8%).
  • Leaf is close to solved; fruit is not. Every model scores 98.56% or higher on leaves, while the best fruit model reaches 78.63%, with anthracnose the hardest class.
  • Pretraining matters on small data. Pretrained models beat the from-scratch hybrids by 5 to 17 points on fruit.

Audit the data first

In the guava data, 139 near-duplicate images carried conflicting labels. They were removed, and remaining twins were kept in one split so no test image has a copy in training.

Test for shortcuts

A classifier that saw only the leaf border reached 81.8% accuracy against 16.7% chance. Backgrounds were removed, and the leaf results held.

Report the uncertainty

Confidence intervals on small test sets, limitations stated in every report, and baselines run on the same data before any claim is made.

PlantSense AI

A farmer photographs a papaya or guava leaf and gets the disease, how to treat it and how to prevent it, in English or Bangla. The photo never leaves the phone.

  • No server to pay for. The papaya model runs in the visitor's browser with ONNX Runtime Web, served as a static site from Vercel's free tier.
  • Built for the field. Full Bangla translation, eight photo formats including iPhone HEIC, light and dark themes, and treatment steps written for farmers.
  • Two crops, two models. MaxViT-Tiny for papaya, and a ConvNeXtV2 model with separable self-attention and background removal for guava.

Embedded · TinyML · IoT Live With Zubayer Ahmad

NanoSense

A wristband that detects a fall on the device itself and texts up to three family members the wearer's vital signs and location. Built for older people, patients recovering at home and anyone who works alone.

82.8%of simulated falls detected (24 of 29, 5-fold CV)
2.9 msper classification on the ESP32 at 240 MHz
923parameters, int8 weights, about 1.3 KB
8×fewer false alarms than a 4 g threshold at the same detection rate
  • The model lives on the wrist. 33 features from the last 2 seconds of motion feed a 33→20→10→3 network written by hand in C, with no ML runtime. A self-test at boot checks the C features against Python.
  • A complete product around it. A family dashboard with live vitals and map, family-code sign-up, SMS password reset, medicine reminders, ring-to-find and firmware updates over WiFi.
  • Works when the internet does not. Detection, alarms and reminders run on the device and settings stay in flash; the cloud only monitors and configures.
